# Calibrating the Ray-Ban Meta camera

**Status: NOT DONE. This camera has never been calibrated.**

This is the one thing standing between World Builder and geometry, and it
cannot be done from a keyboard. It needs a printed board, the real
glasses, and about twenty minutes.

Everything else is built and waiting. On 2026-08-24 a real walk produced
155 keyframes, 36 tracking segments, **0 poses and 0 points** — not
because anything broke, but because `session.intrinsics.source ==
"unknown"`, so `BACKEND_AUTO` selected the backend that withholds every
pose rather than inventing a focal length. That is the correct behaviour.
This document is how you switch it off.

---

## 1. Why there is no shortcut

Every plausible shortcut has been examined and ruled out. None of them is
available, and reopening them wastes a day:

- **The published ~100° FOV.** Third-party sources report 100° for the
  Gen 2 camera; Meta publishes no figure at all. Even taking 100° as
  true, it describes a **3:4 still (3024×4032)**, while our stream is
  **9:16 (360×640)** through an **undocumented crop**. These are
  different sensor readouts and the relationship between them is not
  published anywhere. A focal length derived from one and applied to the
  other is a fabricated number wearing the costume of a measurement.
  (`docs/superpowers/research/2026-08-21-world-builder-readiness.md`
  §5.3.)
- **`fx = k × width` heuristics.** Same objection, with less evidence
  behind it.
- **A default `K` in the classical backend.** `ClassicalTwoViewBackend.
  prepare()` exists in order to *raise* when intrinsics are missing.
- **Marking intrinsics as "guessed".** `tower/world_builder/schema.py`
  defines `unknown`, `self_calibrated` and `declared`, and deliberately
  no fourth value. A guess that can be persisted becomes a guess that
  gets trusted.
- **Reusing a calibration from another resolution.** `records.py`
  refuses to rescale unless `scales_linearly_across_resolutions` is
  established as `True`, and it never has been — whether DAT resizes or
  crops between its three modes is unknown. A calibration wrong by a
  crop factor produces a trajectory that looks plausible and is wrong,
  which is the worst failure mode available.

A wrong focal length is not a small error. It is absorbed almost entirely
by the scale ambiguity, so the reconstruction stays self-consistent and
confident while being wrong. Nothing downstream catches it.

## 2. What you need

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| A printed ChArUco board | Generated by the script; see below |
| Something flat and rigid | Foamboard, clipboard, hardback book, a fridge door. **Not** a hand-held sheet of paper |
| A ruler with millimetres | To verify the print |
| The glasses, the phone, and the Tower | The board must be photographed **through the real pipeline** |

### Generate and print the board

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\calibrate_charuco.py --generate-board board.png
```

The board is **7 × 5 squares, DICT_4X4_50**, and at true scale
(`SQUARE_LENGTH_M = 0.040`) it measures **280 × 200 mm** — which does not
fit A4 or Letter portrait. Three options, in order of preference:

1. **A3 at 100% scale.** Cleanest. 280 × 200 mm sits comfortably inside
   297 × 420 mm.
2. **A4/Letter landscape, scaled down uniformly to fit** (e.g. "fit to
   page" *with aspect ratio locked*). This is fine — see §3.
3. Two A4 sheets taped together. Workable, but the seam must be perfectly
   flat and the two halves perfectly aligned, which is harder than it
   sounds.

Print on matte paper if you have it. Glossy paper throws specular
highlights that kill corner detection under room lighting.

Mount the print **flat and rigid**. A sheet with a bow in it is a curved
target being fitted with a planar model, and the error goes straight into
the distortion coefficients.

## 3. Verify the print — but verify the right thing

**Measured, not assumed:** a *uniform* scale error in the print does
**not** affect the recovered focal length. Calibrating the same 24
synthetic views while declaring the board 25% larger, and again 50%
smaller, than it really is returns a **bit-identical** `fx = 300.689`
(truth 300.0) with a bit-identical RMS. This is expected: uniformly
scaling a planar target scales the recovered *extrinsics* and leaves `K`
untouched. So "print at exactly 40 mm" is not the requirement, and a
common warning to that effect is wrong for intrinsics.

**What actually matters is that the squares are square.** An
*anisotropic* print — the printer stretching one axis relative to the
other — is a real and damaging error, because the calibrator assumes
`SQUARE_LENGTH_M` in both directions. Measured on the same synthetic
views, with the board's X axis stretched and everything else held fixed:

| X stretch | recovered `fx` | `fx` error | recovered `fy` | reprojection RMS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 (correct) | 299.35 | −0.22% | 299.73 | 0.3282 px |
| 1.02 | 300.16 | +0.05% | 295.49 | 0.3337 px |
| 1.05 | 307.52 | **+2.51%** | 294.15 | 0.4565 px |
| 1.10 | 336.18 | **+12.06%** | 306.69 | 0.6722 px |

Note that RMS barely moves. **RMS does not catch this.**

So, the check:

1. Measure the width across all 7 squares, and the height across all 5.
2. Divide: width ÷ 7 and height ÷ 5. **They must agree to within 1%.**
   (At 40 mm squares, that is 0.4 mm — measure across the whole board,
   not one square, so the ruler error divides down.)
3. If they disagree, your printer is scaling non-uniformly. Reprint with
   "actual size" / 100% scaling and page-fitting off.

If, and only if, you also want the board usable for future metric-scale
work, set `SQUARE_LENGTH_M` in `scripts/calibrate_charuco.py` to the size
you actually measured. It changes nothing about the intrinsics this
procedure produces. V1 cannot reach a metric scale by any route
(§8 item 5), so this is optional today.

One thing you must **not** change is the ratio between
`SQUARE_LENGTH_M` and `MARKER_LENGTH_M` (0.040 / 0.030). Printing the
generated PNG at any uniform scale preserves it automatically. Declaring
a wrong ratio measurably degrades the result — in the same probe, a 2:1
ratio against a 4:3 board raised `fx` error from 0.23% to 0.75% and
doubled the RMS.

## 4. Capture the views THROUGH THE GLASSES

This is the part that cannot be substituted. Photographs from the phone
camera are worthless here: they are a different sensor, a different
crop, a different resolution, and a different encoder. **The board must
travel the same path as a mapping frame** — glasses → phone → DAT →
WebSocket → Tower → JPEG on disk — because it is that path's intrinsics
you are measuring, crop and all.

### Arm the recorder and record a dedicated capture

```powershell
$env:TOWER_CAPTURE_ROOT   = "data"
$env:TOWER_WORLD_ROOT     = "data/world_builder"
$env:TOWER_WORLD_AUTOBUILD = "false"    # don't build a junk world from board views
powershell -NoProfile -File scripts\start_tower.ps1
```

`TOWER_WORLD_AUTOBUILD=false` is a convenience, not a requirement: it
just stops a follower attaching and reconstructing a world out of
pictures of a chessboard.

On the phone: open World Builder, press **Start**, capture the views
described below, press **Stop**. The frames land in
`data\captures\<capture_id>\frames\`.

### How to hold the board

The gate that matters is **viewpoint diversity**, not view count, and the
gap is not subtle. Measured before the diversity guard existed:

| Capture | `fx` error | reprojection RMS |
|---|---|---|
| Ten byte-identical views | **287%** | 0.158 px |
| Ten fronto-parallel views, one distance | **3787%** | 0.217 px |
| A correct calibration | 0.2% | **0.253 px** |

Both catastrophic cases came back `source="self_calibrated"`,
`is_known=True`, and **scored a better RMS than the correct
calibration** — because RMS measures fit to the views you supplied, and
degenerate views are trivially easy to fit. Focal length is pinned down
by *perspective foreshortening*; a board held parallel to the sensor
contributes almost nothing toward it no matter how many times you
photograph it.

So:

- **Aim for 25–40 usable views.** The script refuses below 8 distinct
  ones; 8 is the floor, not the target.
- **Tilt the board hard.** At least 8 views should be angled **20–40°**
  away from facing you, about *both* axes — tilt top-away, bottom-away,
  left-away, right-away, and two diagonals. The script requires 4 views
  above a measured tilt threshold that sits between 3° and 9°; a
  3° tilt is still too little to separate focal length from distance.
- **Vary the distance.** Close enough that the board nearly fills the
  frame, out to where it covers maybe a third of it.
- **Move the board around the frame.** Centre, all four corners, both
  edges. This is what pins down the principal point, which is the
  weakly-constrained parameter even on noise-free synthetic input (it
  landed ~2.5 px off truth there, before any real-world effect).
- **Hold each pose still for about a second** before moving to the next.
  You are fighting motion blur and rolling shutter, and a smeared corner
  is a wrong corner, not a missing one.
- Keep the whole board in frame. ChArUco tolerates partial views — that
  is why it is used here rather than a plain checkerboard — but a full
  view contributes more corners.

A slow, deliberate 60–90 seconds is enough. You do not need to cull the
frames: near-duplicate views are collapsed by the script's own
8-pixel-separation rule, and undetectable frames are simply skipped.

### Confirm the recorded resolution before you calibrate

DAT chooses the stream size and can change it mid-stream. The
calibration is valid for exactly one resolution, so check which one you
got:

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -c "import json; from pathlib import Path; ls=[json.loads(l) for l in Path(r'data\captures\<capture_id>\frames.jsonl').read_text().splitlines() if l.strip()]; print(sorted({(r['width'], r['height']) for r in ls}))"
```

You want `[(360, 640)]` — a single entry. Run against the 2026-08-24
capture, this returns exactly `[(360, 640)]` across all **1395** frames,
so a single-resolution capture is the normal case on this rig and not a
lucky outcome. **If more than one size appears, the capture straddled a
DAT ladder step**; keep only the frames at the size you actually stream
at, or recapture. Calibrating across a resolution change silently
averages two different cameras.

Note also that this reads the **journal**, which records the size of the
decoded pixels. `capture.json` and the session's `declared_width` /
`declared_height` record what was *declared*, and on 2026-08-24 the
declaration said 480×360 while every frame was 360×640. Trust the
journal.

## 5. Run the calibration

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\calibrate_charuco.py `
    --frames data\captures\<capture_id>\frames
```

That is the whole command. **Do not pass `--out`.** With no `--out` the
result is written into the intrinsics store at

```
data\world_builder\intrinsics\360x640.json
```

which is exactly where the builder looks it up, keyed by the resolution
your frames *measure* at. The Tower passes `--root <TOWER_WORLD_ROOT>`
to every follower it spawns (`tower/main.py`), and the store lives under
that same root, so **there is no flag for anyone to remember, ever
again**. A file written anywhere else — including with `--out` — is not
discovered, and the script says so when you use it.

If the script **refuses**, it is telling you the truth about your
capture, and the right response is to recapture rather than to argue
with it:

| Refusal | What to do |
|---|---|
| `only N usable views of the board (need 8)` | The board was too small in frame, too blurred, or too badly lit. Get closer, hold still, more light |
| `only N sufficiently distinct viewpoints` | The board barely moved between shots. Move it around the frame and change distance |
| `only N of M views carry meaningful perspective` | The board was held nearly parallel to the sensor. **Tilt it, 20–40°, both axes** |

Nothing is written to the store on a refusal.

## 6. Confirm the builder picked it up

Two checks. Do both.

**a) The store finds it at the resolution you stream at:**

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -c "import logging; logging.basicConfig(level=logging.INFO); from pathlib import Path; from tower.world_builder.intrinsics_store import IntrinsicsStore; print(IntrinsicsStore(Path('data/world_builder')).lookup(360, 640))"
```

You want `source='self_calibrated'` and a real `fx`. Today, before any
calibration exists, it prints the miss and then
`CameraIntrinsics(source='unknown', ...)` with every field `None` —
which is the correct current answer. The `basicConfig` is there so the
store's own reasoning reaches the console; if you get `unknown` *after*
calibrating, that log line names the path it checked, and the most
likely cause is that the calibration was measured at a different
resolution than the one you asked for.

**b) A real build selects the classical backend.** Run a session over an
already-recorded capture and read the first two log lines:

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\world_build_session.py `
    --frames data\captures\<capture_id>\frames --name "calibration check"
```

Before calibration you get:

```
WARNING ... NO CALIBRATION for the observed 360x640 frames (looked for
  ...\intrinsics\360x640.json). Intrinsics stay unknown, so the unposed
  backend runs and this session will produce 0 poses and 0 points.
...
backend_id  unposed
downgraded_from  classical
```

After calibration you get:

```
INFO ... using calibration ...\intrinsics\360x640.json --
  source=self_calibrated 360x640 fx=... fy=... reprojection_rms=... px views=...
...
backend_id  classical-sfm
downgraded_from  None
```

`backend_id: classical-sfm` with `downgraded_from: null` is the switch
having been thrown. The per-edge record is the deeper confirmation:
before calibration every edge in `edges.jsonl` carries `degeneracy:
"no_intrinsics"` — meaning no geometric gate was ever *evaluated*. After,
you see real geometric verdicts (`low_parallax`, `pure_rotation`,
`none`), which is the pipeline actually doing geometry.

## 7. Telling a good calibration from a bad one that scored well

**Do not use reprojection RMS as the quality gate.** §4's table is the
whole argument: the two catastrophic calibrations both beat a correct one
on RMS. RMS tells you the solver converged on the views you gave it. It
says nothing about whether those views could constrain the answer.

Use these instead, in order:

**1. View count and diversity.** `view_count` well above the floor of 8
— 20 or more. If it is 8 or 9, you scraped through.

**2. `fx ≈ fy`.** Modern sensors have square pixels. Agreement within
~2% is expected; a 10% gap means something is wrong, most likely an
anisotropic print (§3) or a mixed-resolution capture (§4).

**3. `cx, cy` near the frame centre.** For 360×640, expect roughly
(180, 320). A few percent off is normal and legitimate — the readout may
be an off-centre crop, and this is the weakest parameter in the fit. More
than ~15% off, or outside the frame entirely, means recapture.

**4. Split-half agreement — the real test.** Calibrate twice from
disjoint halves of your views and compare. If the views genuinely
constrain the camera, both halves land in the same place:

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\calibrate_charuco.py `
    --frames data\captures\<capture_id>\frames --split-half
```

```
half A  fx=  300.60  fy=  300.99  rms=0.2822  views=12
half B  fx=  301.55  fy=  301.98  rms=0.2229  views=12

fx disagreement between halves: 0.31%
```

It writes nothing — it is purely a diagnostic. If either half cannot be
calibrated at all, that refusal is itself the answer: the capture does
not carry enough independent evidence.

Measured on synthetic views: **0.31%** on a well-varied 24-view set,
**1.94%** on another, and **4.55%** on a deliberately marginal set with
small tilts and little depth variation — whose *full-set* `fx` error was
nevertheless only 0.13%, and whose RMS (0.3806) was indistinguishable
from the good one's (0.3660). The spread caught the weakness that neither
`fx` nor RMS revealed.

**Treat >5% spread as a failed calibration and recapture.** Note the
honest limit: split-half detects *under-constraint*. It cannot detect a
systematically wrong board, because both halves share the same board.
That is what §3's measurement is for.

**5. The world it builds.** The final check is a real walk producing
poses that go where you walked. Nothing above substitutes for it.

Record whatever you conclude against §8 below — the first real calibration
is the answer to an open question, and it should stop being open.

## 8. What is still unknown after this is done

Calibrating removes one blocker. It does not remove these, and none of
them should be quietly assumed away:

1. **Whether this procedure works at all on real hardware.**
   `calibrate_charuco.py` **has never seen a printed board.** Its code
   path is unit-tested against synthetically rendered views and recovers
   a known focal length to within ~0.3%, which validates the wiring —
   genuinely worth having, since `calibrateCameraCharuco` does not exist
   in OpenCV 5 and the working sequence is `CharucoDetector` →
   `matchImagePoints` → `calibrateCamera`. **Every number in this
   document is synthetic.** The first real run may refuse, may fail to
   detect the board at all under real lighting and JPEG compression, or
   may succeed. Nobody knows which.
2. **The distortion model.** The script emits `pinhole_radtan`. On a
   ~100° wearable lens that may be inadequate and a fisheye model may be
   required. The synthetic input is a perfect pinhole with **zero**
   distortion, so this — the parameter most likely to be wrong — is
   completely unexercised. If your board's straight edges look bowed in
   the captured frames, radtan is on trial.
3. **Rolling shutter.** Not modelled anywhere, by anything.
4. **Every other DAT resolution.** The calibration is stamped
   `scales_linearly_across_resolutions: null` and will refuse to rescale.
   A session at any other size behaves exactly as if you had never
   calibrated. To cover the ladder you must calibrate at each rung —
   and calibrating at two rungs is also how you would *establish*
   linearity, rather than assume it.
5. **Metric scale.** Calibration does not deliver it. V1 reaches only
   `unknown` and `relative` scale states; `estimated` and `measured` are
   unreachable and `format_distance` refuses to print metres. A
   calibrated monocular reconstruction is internally consistent in an
   arbitrary unit.
6. **Whether the delivered resolution supports reconstruction at all.**
   360×640 through a lossy encoder is not much to triangulate from, and
   that question is downstream of this one.
